Defining Photosynthesis

PHOTO = Light

SYNTHESIS = Composition/Construction

Photosynthesis: The conversion of light energy into chemical energy

5 / 17

Process Classification

Photosynthesis is characterized as:

  • Anabolic - builds complex molecules from simpler ones
  • Endothermic - requires energy input
  • Photoautotrophic - uses light as energy source

Plants are primary producers in ecosystems

6 / 17

Process Requirements

Essential components:

  • Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) - atmospheric source
  • Water (H₂O) - absorbed through roots
  • Light Energy - electromagnetic spectrum (400-700nm)

Plus: Chlorophyll - light-absorbing pigment in chloroplasts

7 / 17

Site of Photosynthesis

Where does photosynthesis occur?

Primary location: Mesophyll cells in leaves

Specifically within chloroplasts containing chlorophyll

Chlorophyll functions as a light-harvesting complex, absorbing photons and converting light energy into chemical energy.

8 / 17

Process Mechanism

  1. CO₂ uptake - diffusion through stomata
  2. H₂O transport - xylem-mediated delivery
  3. Light absorption - chlorophyll excitation
  4. Energy conversion - synthesis of glucose
  5. O₂ release - diffusion to atmosphere

Chemical Equation

Carbon Dioxide + Water + Light Energy

↓

GLUCOSE + OXYGEN

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Glucose Utilization

Metabolic fates of synthesized glucose:

  • Cellular respiration - ATP production for metabolic processes
  • Biosynthesis - formation of cellulose, proteins, lipids
  • Storage - conversion to starch for later use

Research Findings

Global Oxygen

Photosynthetic organisms produce >99% of atmospheric oxygen

Marine Contribution

Phytoplankton account for ~50% of global photosynthesis

Carbon Fixation

~120 billion tons of carbon fixed annually

Evolutionary History

Process evolved ~3.5 billion years ago in cyanobacteria
